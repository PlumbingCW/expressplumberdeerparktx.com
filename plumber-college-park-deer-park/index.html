What a mid-century neighborhood means for plumbing

A median build year of 1967 tells you the neighborhood is established. It does not tell you what is inside any wall. Some homes have had their plumbing updated, some have not, and the only way to know is to look. A plumber can check supply lines, shutoff valves and drain connections and tell you what condition they are actually in.

For drains, the most useful step is often a camera look. A sewer camera shows the inside of the line, so you see whether the problem is a clog, a bellied section, roots or a break before anyone starts digging. That is general plumbing practice and not a claim about College Park lines.

Flat ground and slow drainage

The City of Deer Park describes the area as flat with soils that drain poorly, and notes that you do not need to be in a floodplain to flood. For a homeowner the practical meaning is simple. Water that cannot run away fast tends to sit, so a leak near the foundation or a wet patch in the yard after dry weather deserves a look sooner than you might think. That is not a finding about College Park in particular.
