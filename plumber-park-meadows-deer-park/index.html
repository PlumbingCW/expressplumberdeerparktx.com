Hidden leaks and the meter test

A hidden leak rarely announces itself. It shows up as a bill that climbs for no reason, a faint sound of running water or a damp spot that does not dry. The meter test is the quickest check. Turn off every fixture, read the meter and watch the leak indicator. If it moves, water is escaping.

Close the house valve and look again. If the indicator stops, the leak is inside your property. If it keeps moving, the leak is on the line before the valve.

If you see water coming up in the street or at the meter, Public Works is the city contact: 281-478-7270 in business hours, 281-479-1511 after hours. A leak at or before the meter is the city's to sort out, while a leak on your side belongs to a plumber.

After a boil water notice

Deer Park issued a boil water notice in February 2024. The city reports that its water is disinfected with chlorine and comes from the Coastal Water Authority's Trinity River supply. If a notice is ever in force, follow the city's instructions first. Plumbing questions about water that tastes or smells different are separate from a notice, so call Public Works if you suspect the supply and a plumber if you suspect the lines inside your house.
