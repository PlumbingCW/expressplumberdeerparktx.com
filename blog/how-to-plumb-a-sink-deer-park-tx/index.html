How to Plumb a Sink in Deer Park, TX

Plumbing a sink means connecting hot and cold supply lines to a faucet and connecting the drain basket to a P-trap that leads to the wall. The steps are the same for a bathroom or a kitchen sink. The parts you need are shutoff valves, two supply lines, a faucet, a drain assembly, a trap and a few fittings. A confident homeowner can do it in an afternoon, but permit rules apply in Deer Park.

Parts and tools you need

Gather before you start

  • ✓A faucet and its mounting hardware, with the manufacturer's installation sheet.
  • ✓Two braided supply lines sized to reach from the shutoff valves to the faucet.
  • ✓Quarter-turn shutoff valves if the existing ones are old or stiff.
  • ✓A drain assembly, a P-trap and a trap adapter that matches the wall pipe.
  • ✓Plumber's tape, an adjustable wrench, a basin wrench and a bucket.

The steps

1

Shut off and drain

Close the main water valve, open the old faucet to drain the lines and set a bucket under the trap.

2

Install the faucet

Mount the faucet to the sink or counter, then tighten the mounting nuts from below with a basin wrench.

3

Install the drain basket

Seal the drain flange to the sink with plumber's putty or the gasket supplied, then tighten the nut underneath.

4

Connect supply lines

Attach the braided lines from the faucet to the shutoff valves, hand-tight plus a quarter turn with a wrench.

5

Assemble the trap

Fit the tailpiece, the P-trap and the trap arm to the wall drain, then check that all slip nuts are snug and aligned.

6

Test for leaks

Open the valves slowly, run the faucet and fill the basin. Dry every joint with a paper towel and look for dampness.

Do not mix thread types. Supply line nuts, drain slip nuts and compression fittings use different washers. If the package says the washer is included, check that it sits flat before you tighten.

Where homeowners get stuck

Old shutoff valves that will not close

A seized valve is a common reason a simple swap becomes a plumber call. Replace the valve.

Wall pipe that does not line up

If the wall drain is higher or lower than the trap arm, an adapter or an extension is needed.

Corroded galvanized pipe

Old threaded pipe can crack when turned. Stop and call a plumber.

Slow drain after installation

A trap that slopes the wrong way or a vent problem. See plumbing installation.

Permit rules in Deer Park

Plumbing permits and homeowner work

The City of Deer Park lists a plumbing permit for all plumbing work, with water heaters, re-piping, sewer work and irrigation named as examples. A homeowner may pull a plumbing permit for work on a primary residence with a Harris County homestead exemption, but only the homeowner may do the work. If a contractor does it, the contractor must pull the permit. Unpermitted work carries a $500 penalty plus triple the permit fee under Ordinance 3392. Call the Permit Office at 281-479-2394 to confirm whether a like-for-like fixture swap needs one.

For a rental, ask the landlord first. Many leases forbid tenant plumbing work, and a permit requires the owner's involvement.

When to hand it to a plumber

Call for help if the shutoff valves will not close, the drain pipe is corroded, a new fixture needs a new vent or the sink is moving to a different wall. These are the moments a small project grows. Our 24/7 line takes planning questions as well as emergencies.

Testing and troubleshooting after the install

1

Fill and drain the basin

Fill the sink to the overflow and release the stopper. Water should leave steadily with no gurgling.

2

Check each joint

Run a dry paper towel around every nut and fitting. A damp spot means a connection needs another quarter turn.

3

Check again after an hour

Slow drips often appear only after pressure has built in the line for a while.

4

Look again tomorrow

A final check the next day catches leaks that take time to show.

A slow drip at a supply nut usually means the washer is not seated. Shut the valve, loosen the nut, reseat the washer and retighten. A leak at a slip joint on the trap means the washer is misaligned or the nut is cross-threaded, and starting over is better than tightening harder.
