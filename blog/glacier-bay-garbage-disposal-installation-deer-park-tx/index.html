Glacier Bay Garbage Disposal Installation: A Deer Park, TX Homeowner Guide

Installing a garbage disposal such as a Glacier Bay model means removing the old unit, mounting the new one under the sink flange, connecting the drain and the dishwasher hose and wiring or plugging in power. Follow the manufacturer's instruction sheet for the exact mounting parts. The job is manageable for a careful homeowner, but electrical rules matter in Deer Park.

Before you start

Gather and check

  • ✓The new disposal, its mounting hardware and the manufacturer's instruction sheet.
  • ✓A power cord kit if your unit does not come with one and the wall outlet is under the sink.
  • ✓Plumber's putty, a screwdriver, pliers, a bucket and a flashlight.
  • ✓Safety glasses and gloves.
  • ✓A way to confirm power is off, either the breaker or a non-contact tester.

Turn off the disposal at the wall switch and the circuit at the panel. Unplug the unit or disconnect the wires inside the junction box. Never put your hand in a disposal, even when it is off.

Removing the old unit

1

Clear the cabinet

Remove items and place a bucket under the trap.

2

Disconnect the drain and dishwasher hose

Loosen the drain pipe and the dishwasher hose clamp.

3

Release the mounting ring

Twist or unscrew the ring according to the mounting style. Support the unit as it comes free.

4

Clean the sink flange

Remove old putty and check the flange for cracks.

Mounting the new disposal

1

Seat the flange

Press fresh plumber's putty under the sink flange and set it into the drain opening.

2

Assemble the mount

Slide the gasket, backup ring and mounting ring onto the flange from below, as the sheet shows.

3

Attach the disposal

Lift the unit into place and lock it to the mounting ring.

4

Remove the dishwasher plug

If a dishwasher connects here, knock out the plug inside the dishwasher inlet with a screwdriver and remove the plug from the unit. Skipping this blocks drainage.

5

Connect the drain and hose

Reconnect the discharge tube to the trap and the dishwasher hose to the inlet.

Electrical permits in Deer Park

The City of Deer Park says any type of residential electrical work requires a permit and that electrical permits are normally pulled by registered licensed electricians. Homeowners are an exception for work on their own primary residence with a Harris County homestead exemption. If your disposal needs a new circuit, outlet or hardwired connection, check with the Public Works Permit Office at 281-479-2394 before you start. A plumber can handle the plumbing side and coordinate with an electrician for the wiring.

Source type: City of Deer Park Building Permits and Contractors pages

Test and troubleshoot

Restore power, run cold water and switch the disposal on for a few seconds. Look under the sink for drips at the flange, the drain and the dishwasher hose. Check again after the first day.

If the unit hums but does not turn, it may have shipped jammed. Use the hex key socket on the bottom, then press the reset button. If water leaks at the flange, tighten the mounting screws evenly. If it leaks at the bottom of the unit, the housing is defective and should go back.

When to call a plumber

DIY is reasonable when

  • The new unit matches the old mount style
  • A grounded outlet and a switch already exist
  • The drain pipe and shutoff valves are in good shape
  • You are comfortable turning off breakers and testing for power

Call a plumber or electrician when

  • The sink drain is corroded or the trap does not line up
  • No outlet exists and wiring must be run
  • The old unit has leaked into the cabinet floor
  • You want a warranty-backed installation

Choosing and sizing a disposal

Motor size, feed type and sound insulation are the main differences between models. A continuous-feed unit runs while the switch is on, and a batch-feed unit runs only with the stopper in place. Buy the one that fits how your household cooks.

Check the cabinet first. Measure the space below the flange, because a larger motor means a taller unit and a deeper trap arm. A tight cabinet may need a compact model or a different trap layout.
