Causes of Slab Leaks in Residential Plumbing: Deer Park, TX Guide

Slab leaks happen when a pressurized water line under the foundation wears through, cracks or loses a joint. The usual causes are corrosion of the pipe, constant rubbing against concrete or soil, high water pressure, poor installation and, in some cases, movement of the ground around the foundation. Knowing the cause matters because it decides whether to patch one spot or replace the line.

The main causes, ranked by how often we hear about them

Corrosion from age

Copper and galvanized steel thin from the inside. Pinholes open where the wall is weakest.

Abrasion

A pipe vibrating against concrete, a rock or another pipe slowly wears through. Pressure surges make it worse.

High water pressure

Pressure above what the fixtures and pipe were rated for strains every joint.

Installation flaws

Poorly crimped, soldered or glued joints and kinked or unsupported pipe can fail years later.

Soil and chemistry

Water in contact with soil that is acidic or full of minerals can eat at metal pipe.

Ground movement

Shifting soil can bend or crack a line in the slab. It is less common than the others.

What the local record supports and what it does not

It is tempting to name one local cause for every leak. We try not to. Deer Park's own city pages describe the area as flat, with poorly draining soils, and note that you do not have to be in a floodplain to flood. Those facts help explain why leak water lingers, but they do not prove what caused any single leak.

The Harris-Galveston Subsidence District explains that long-term groundwater pumping compacts clay and silt layers and has lowered land across the region. Its brochure links subsidence to flooding and infrastructure strain. It does not say that subsidence breaks house plumbing, and neither can we say it did in your home.

Deer Park's housing age

The City of Deer Park's history page shows the town platted in 1892, the Shell refinery arriving in 1928, incorporation in 1948 and annexation by neighboring cities in 1965 that left it unable to grow outward. The oldest marked home dates to 1928. The city does not publish a housing age breakdown, so we cannot say how old your home's pipes are. In general an older supply line is more likely to be corroded, so age is one cause a plumber considers when looking at a slab leak.

Reducing your risk

What a homeowner can do

  • ✓Have the water pressure checked. A gauge on an outdoor spigot takes a minute.
  • ✓Replace a failing pressure regulator before it passes high pressure to every pipe.
  • ✓Watch the water bill and the meter for slow climbs.
  • ✓Know where the main shutoff is and test it once a year.
  • ✓If you see repeated pinhole leaks, ask a plumber about a reroute before the next one.

No habit prevents a slab leak, but early detection limits the damage. A leak found while it is small wets a few feet of floor, and one found late can soak a room and the soil beneath it. See slab leak repair for the repair options and leak detection for how we locate the line.

When a slab leak becomes a repiping question

One leak on a sound line is a repair. Three leaks in two years on the same line is a pattern. At that point each patch buys less time, and the cost of repeated floor openings can pass the cost of a reroute. A plumber should show you the evidence, such as pitting at the cut, before recommending anything larger.

Ask how the new line would run. A reroute through the attic or walls avoids breaking the slab at all, which is often the point. Ask also how long water will be off and whether the house stays livable during the work.
