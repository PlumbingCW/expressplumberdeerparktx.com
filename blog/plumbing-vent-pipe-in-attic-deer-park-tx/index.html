Plumbing Vent Pipe in the Attic for Deer Park, TX Homes

A plumbing vent pipe runs from your drain lines up through the attic and out the roof. It lets air into the system so water drains smoothly and lets sewer gas escape above the roof instead of into the house. When a vent is blocked, cracked or disconnected, you get gurgling drains, slow fixtures and sewer smells. Leaks around the roof penetration can also stain attic ceilings.

What the vent does

Every drain needs air behind the water. Without it, water leaving a pipe creates suction that pulls the water out of the trap, the curved piece under each fixture. The trap normally holds a plug of water that blocks sewer gas. A vent breaks the suction and keeps the trap full.

The vent stack tees into the drain lines near each fixture, rises inside the wall into the attic and exits through the roof. Several fixtures often share a vent, and in an attic you will see the vertical pipe, tees where branches join and sometimes a horizontal run.

Signs a vent is the problem

Gurgling from drains

Air is pulled backward through the trap when water drains, which makes a gurgle.

Slow drains with no clog

A partly blocked vent can make water drain slowly even when the pipe is open.

Sewer smell in the bathroom

A dry trap or a cracked vent lets gas into the house.

Toilets that flush weakly

Air cannot enter behind the water, which weakens the siphon.

Stains on the ceiling near the roof penetration

A cracked rubber boot or flashing leaks around the pipe.

What you can safely check

From the ground and the attic hatch

  • ✓From the yard, look at the roof pipe for obvious blockage or a bird nest. Do not climb a roof you are not comfortable on.
  • ✓In the attic, use a flashlight to follow the vertical pipe and look for cracks, disconnected joints or damp insulation.
  • ✓Check the underside of the roof sheathing around the pipe for water stains.
  • ✓Run water at the fixtures that gurgle, and listen for where the sound seems to come from.

If you see damage, do not patch a drain or vent pipe with tape. A cracked vent leaks sewer gas into the attic, which can enter living space. Call for repair.

Tropical rain season and roof penetrations

Deer Park's storm calendar

The City of Deer Park's flood hazard page says September is typically the most active month for tropical activity and that the city has seen major flooding from Tropical Storm Allison in 2001 and Tropical Storm Erin in 2007. A rubber vent boot that has dried and cracked can leak in a heavy rain, so inspecting roof penetrations before the tropical season is a sensible habit. Water entering through a vent boot shows up as stains on attic framing and ceilings.

Source type: City of Deer Park Know Your Flood Hazard page

A new roof boot costs little compared with a ceiling repair. If your roof is aging, ask the roofer to replace the vent boots at the same time. We handle the plumbing side if the pipe itself is cracked.

Vent trouble is easy to misdiagnose from the sound alone. A gurgle that appears only when the washing machine drains is a different problem from one that appears when you flush. Tell the plumber which fixture is running when the sound starts. It tells us which branch to test first.

What to do before you go into the attic

Attic safety checklist

  • ✓Do not enter a hot attic for long. Go early in the day and take water with you.
  • ✓Step only on joists or boards, never on bare insulation or drywall.
  • ✓Do not touch wiring, and do not handle old insulation without a mask and gloves.
  • ✓Tell someone you are going up, and bring a good light.
  • ✓Call a plumber for vent work that needs cutting, since a bad vent connection can let sewer gas into the house.
