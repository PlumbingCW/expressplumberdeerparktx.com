Tankless Hot Water Heater Not Getting Hot in Deer Park, TX Homes

A tankless water heater that does not get hot is usually dealing with scale on the heat exchanger, a clogged inlet filter, low water flow, a gas or power supply fault or a crossover between hot and cold lines. Start by checking the display for an error code, then confirm the unit has power or gas and clean the inlet filter. If the unit still runs cold, a plumber should test it.

Start with the display and the basics

Five checks before you call

  • ✓Read the display for an error code and look it up in the manual. Take a photo of it for the plumber.
  • ✓Confirm the unit has power, or for gas units that the gas valve is open and other gas appliances work.
  • ✓Check that the water valves to the heater are fully open.
  • ✓Clean the cold-water inlet filter, which can clog and cut flow.
  • ✓Try a second tap. If only one fixture is cold, the issue is local to that fixture or its pipe.

Common causes in order of likelihood

Scale on the heat exchanger

Mineral buildup acts like insulation and limits heat transfer. A descaling flush often restores heat.

Flow too low to fire

Most tankless units need a minimum flow rate to start. A low-flow faucet or a partly clogged aerator can keep the burner from lighting.

Flow too high

Several fixtures running at once can exceed the unit's capacity, so the temperature drops.

Cross-connection

A failed mixing valve or a faulty single-lever faucet can mix cold into the hot line. The heater is fine and the fixture is not.

Gas or ignition faults

A dirty flame sensor, low gas pressure or a blocked vent triggers a lockout.

Failed sensors or board

Temperature sensors and control boards wear out and send wrong readings.

Hot at one tap but not another

If the shower is cold but the kitchen sink is hot, the water heater is working. The shower valve cartridge may be mixing cold water, or a distant fixture may have a long run of cooled pipe. If you hear the heater fire at one tap and not another, the trouble is on the flow side, not the heat side.

A fixture that is cold only when another is open can also mean the pressure balance is off. That calls for a plumber to check the valves and, in some homes, a pressure regulator.

What a plumber checks

1

Read the fault history

The control board stores recent errors, which narrow the cause.

2

Test flow and temperature rise

A flow meter and thermometer show whether the unit meets its rating.

3

Inspect combustion and venting

A gas unit needs clean combustion and a clear flue.

4

Check the plumbing around it

Valves, filters, recirculation lines and the crossover test.

If the heat exchanger is scaled or a part has failed, water heater repair covers parts and labor. If the unit is old and the repair cost is high, a replacement may make more sense, and water heater replacement covers that path.

Preventing the problem

Habits that keep a tankless unit hot

  • ✓Flush the heat exchanger once a year or as the manual says.
  • ✓Clean the inlet filter every few months, and more often if you see debris.
  • ✓Keep the vent and air intake clear of lint, nests and stored items.
  • ✓Keep the manual and the service records near the unit.
  • ✓Replace aerators that are clogged, since they can cut flow below the unit's minimum.
