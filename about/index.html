How we handle trust

We do not post star ratings, review counts or license numbers on this site, because we have not verified them for publication here. Texas licenses plumbers through the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners, and you can check any plumber's license yourself through the board's public license search or by calling (512) 936-5200.

That check is worth doing with any contractor. A plumber who is happy to give a name and a license number when asked is easier to trust than a page that claims credentials it does not show.

Permits in Deer Park

The City of Deer Park requires a plumbing permit for all plumbing work, including water heater replacement, repiping and sewer work. Contractors register with the city and carry insurance, and unpermitted work can bring a $500 penalty plus triple the permit fee under city ordinance. The Public Works Permit Office at City Hall is reachable at 281-479-2394. Ask any plumber whether the permit is part of the quote.

The kinds of homes and calls we see

Deer Park is a mix of older neighborhoods and newer streets. The city's history page puts the original plat at 1892, the Shell refinery at 1928 and incorporation at 1948, and lists its oldest marked home at 1928. The city does not publish a housing age breakdown, so we do not assume what is behind your walls. The calls we take are the usual ones: leaking supply lines, tired water heaters and drains that have been slowing for years.

The city also describes its terrain as flat, with poorly draining soils. That does not decide any single plumbing problem, but it is one reason we ask where water is going and whether it can drain away before we suggest a fix.
