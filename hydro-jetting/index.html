Why a cable sometimes is not enough

A drain cable pokes a hole through a stoppage so water can flow again. The ring of grease, scale or root hair that built up along the pipe wall stays behind and grows back. A line that clogs every few months is usually a line that was never fully cleaned.

A hydro jetter feeds a flexible hose with a high-pressure nozzle through the line. Jets angled backward pull the hose forward and blast the wall with water at thousands of pounds per square inch. The debris flushes downstream to the main instead of sitting in the pipe.

When hydro jetting is an emergency

Call the 24/7 line if sewage is backing into tubs or floor drains and a cable has already failed once. Hydro jetting can restore flow on a fully blocked main when a cable cannot get through, and we dispatch same-day when appointments are available.

Keep every drain unused while you wait. Water sent into a blocked line has nowhere to go but back into the lowest fixture in the house.

What we check before we jet

1

Camera first

We run a sewer camera inspection through a cleanout to see the pipe material, any offsets and what the blockage is made of.

2

Match nozzle to pipe

A rotating nozzle for grease and scale, a penetrating nozzle for packed blockages and a root-cutting head if roots are present.

3

Control the flow

We jet from the cleanout downstream so debris moves toward the main instead of backing up into the house.

4

Verify with a second pass

A final camera run confirms the wall is clean and shows any damage jetting uncovered.

When we do not jet

Jetting is for sound pipe. A cracked, collapsed or heavily offset line can be damaged further by high-pressure water, which is why the camera comes first. If the video shows a break, the repair conversation moves to sewer line repair and jetting waits.

Older cast iron with heavy tuberculation needs a careful approach, because scale can hold a worn pipe together. We discuss the risk before we start.

Lines that benefit most in Deer Park

Homes and buildings with older cast iron or clay laterals can have rough pipe walls that collect grease. Restaurants and food service tenants along Spencer Highway and the Highway 225 corridor produce grease at a rate that no cable keeps up with. Commercial plumbing service often includes a scheduled jetting plan for that reason.

Roots find joints in older clay pipe and grow into it for moisture. A jetter with a cutting nozzle clears them, though roots return if the joint stays open. A camera shows which joints need repair, and we price that separately.
