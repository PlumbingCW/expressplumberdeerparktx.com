A faucet or valve you cannot shut off

Under most sinks are two small stop valves, one for hot and one for cold. Turning them clockwise stops water to that faucet. If a stop valve will not turn, or it leaks when you try, close the main water valve for the house and call.

We handle faucet and valve emergencies 24/7 and book same-day appointments when available. A seized stop valve is a repair we do often, and replacing it can take under an hour on the right fixture.

Kitchen, bath, shower and outdoor

Kitchen faucets

Pull-down sprayers and single-handle cartridges. Hose and diverter faults are common.

Bath and tub faucets

Tub spouts and two-handle sets wear out stems and seats. A running tub spout often means a bad diverter.

Shower valves

A dripping shower head or a hot-only handle points to a worn cartridge inside the wall valve.

Outdoor spigots

A drip at the handle or a leak behind the wall. A cracked spigot can leak inside the wall for weeks.

Shower valves are the more involved of these because they sit behind tile. We often replace the cartridge from the front, without opening the wall. If the valve body itself has failed, we discuss access options before we cut anything.

Repair, replace or upgrade

Repair the faucet when

  • The body is solid and the finish is intact
  • The part you need is a common cartridge or seal
  • The cost is a small share of a new faucet
  • You like the style

Replace the faucet when

  • The finish is pitted or the body is corroded
  • Parts are no longer made
  • You want a pull-down sprayer or touchless model
  • Repair cost approaches replacement cost

Stop valves and supply lines: the small parts that flood cabinets

The braided supply lines under a sink are rated for years of service, not decades. A line that is kinked, corroded at the nut or bulging at the braid can fail without warning, usually in the middle of the night when pressure is highest. If your lines are more than about ten years old, replacing them when the faucet is serviced is cheap insurance.

Stop valves are the other weak point. Many of them have never been turned since the house was built, which is why they seize. Turn each one off and on once a year so it works when you need it.
