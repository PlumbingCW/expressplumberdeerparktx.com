What a sewer camera inspection shows

A camera inspection is the only reliable way to know the condition of a line buried under a lawn or slab. A waterproof camera on a flexible cable travels through the pipe and sends live video to a monitor, where the plumber and you can see cracks, root intrusion, offsets, standing water and buildup.

A locator in the camera head transmits a signal we can follow above ground. That lets us mark depth and position if a repair is needed.

When to schedule one

Before buying a home

A general home inspection rarely includes the sewer lateral. A camera look can catch an expensive problem before closing.

After repeated clogs

Two clogs in a year point to a cause. The camera finds it.

Before a repair

A video guides the method, whether spot repair, lining or replacement.

After a flood or heavy rain

Water, silt and shifting soil can damage lines. A look confirms whether the line is sound.

Timing an inspection around a home purchase

Schedule the camera visit during the option period or inspection window of your contract, while you can still negotiate. The seller then has a chance to repair the line, offer a credit or adjust the price. Bring a copy of the video to your agent.

If the footage is clear, you gain confidence in the line. If it shows roots or a break, you learn the repair method and the cost before you own the house. Either result is more useful than finding out after a backup.
