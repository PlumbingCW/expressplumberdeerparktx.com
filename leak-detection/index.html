When a hidden leak is an emergency

A leak that is soaking drywall, dripping through a ceiling or running under a slab is an emergency, even if you cannot see the source yet. If the stain is growing or the water is near light fixtures or outlets, close the main valve and call us any hour.

We book same-day appointments when they are available. If you can, take a photo of the meter dial and the stain before shutting the water off, so the plumber sees what you saw.

Start with the meter test

1

Turn everything off

Close taps, stop the washer and dishwasher and make sure no one flushes a toilet.

2

Read the meter

Find the low-flow indicator, a small triangle or dial on most meters. If it moves with all fixtures off, water is leaking somewhere.

3

Shut the main valve

Close the house shutoff and check the meter again. If it still moves, the leak is on the supply line between the meter and the house.

4

Call with the result

Tell the dispatcher what you saw. It tells us where to look first.

That simple test sorts leaks into two groups: inside the house and underground in the yard. Each group calls for different tools.

How we find a leak without opening the wall

Acoustic listening

A sensitive microphone on the floor, wall or pipe picks up the hiss of water escaping under pressure.

Thermal imaging

A thermal camera shows a hot spot on a floor or a cool patch on a wall that points to a hot or cold water line.

Pressure testing

We isolate a line, pressurize it and watch the gauge. A drop confirms the leak is on that section.

Tracer gas and moisture meters

Used when other tools are inconclusive. A moisture meter maps how far water has spread.

High water bill? Check these first

Quick checks before we arrive

  • ✓Listen for running toilets. A flapper that does not seal can waste hundreds of gallons a day.
  • ✓Check outdoor spigots and irrigation zones for steady drips or wet ground.
  • ✓Compare the last three bills. A sudden step up points to a new leak, a gradual climb points to wear.
  • ✓Mark the meter reading, wait an hour with no use and read it again.

Leaks in Deer Park's flat, slow-draining ground

Source note: why leaks linger

The City of Deer Park's flood hazard page cites flat topography and poorly draining soils across the city. Water from an underground leak has few places to run off, so it can sit near the pipe and soak soil for a long time before surfacing. A wet yard after a dry week is worth a meter test.
