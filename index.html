When to call at night

What counts as a plumbing emergency

Call the emergency line when water is moving where it should not be, or when something cannot be used safely. Waiting until morning turns a small repair into drywall, flooring and mold work, and in Deer Park's flat terrain a backed-up line can spread across a slab-on-grade floor quickly.

Water you cannot stop

A burst pipe, a failed supply line or a valve that will not close. Shut the main valve first, then call.

Sewage coming back up

Toilets gurgling, tubs filling when the washer drains or sewage at a floor drain. Stop using water in the house.

A tank leaking or hot water gone

A water heater leaking from the base should be shut off at the cold-water inlet. A cold shower alone can usually wait for the morning dispatch window, but a leak cannot.

A meter that keeps spinning

Every fixture is off and the dial still moves. The leak is real, and the bill is climbing while you read this.

Local knowledge

Deer Park homes and the plumbing they hide

Deer Park grew up around the Shell refinery that broke ground in 1928, and the town voted to incorporate on December 11, 1948. Annexation by neighboring cities in 1965 froze the city's boundaries, so most growth since has been infill. The city does not publish a housing age breakdown, so we do not guess what is behind your walls. If your home is more than a few decades old, ask a plumber to note the pipe material during any repair visit.

The oldest home the city has marked dates to 1928 on James Street. We carry both modern PEX and the fittings to tie into existing copper and galvanized runs, so a repair does not force a bigger remodel than the leak requires.

Drinking water in Deer Park is surface water the city buys from Houston through the Coastal Water Authority, treated at the city's own surface water treatment plant. Its 2023 Water Quality Report lists chlorine as the disinfectant, and chlorine can wear out rubber flappers and seals over time, which is a common reason toilets run. The report does not publish a hardness figure, so test your own tap before sizing any softener.

Water that does not stay in the pipes is a flood-planning issue too. The city's floodplain office notes that you do not have to be in a floodplain to flood, and the flat, poorly draining soils are part of why. After a heavy rain, check your cleanouts and floor drains before they back up.

Before you hire

Permits, licensing and who to call first

Two numbers worth saving

For a leak in the street or at the meter, the city's Public Works line is 281-478-7270 during business hours and 281-479-1511 after hours. For sewer trouble, Public Works asks you to call before hiring a plumber, because crews will clear a blockage if it is in the city main. If it is on your side of the property line, call us.

Texas licenses plumbers through the State Board of Plumbing Examiners, and you can check any plumber's license on the Board's public license search. Deer Park also requires a plumbing permit for work such as water heaters, re-piping and sewer work, and the city says unpermitted work carries a $500 penalty plus triple the permit fee. We pull the permit on jobs that need one.

Prices depend on what is behind the wall. A faucet repair is one line item, while a slab leak under a concrete floor is another. We quote the actual job before work starts and explain what moves the number.
