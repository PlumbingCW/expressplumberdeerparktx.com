When a water line break is urgent

A break in the service line sends pressurized water into the soil. You might see a spring of water in the yard, a sinkhole in the lawn or a meter that moves nonstop. Any of these is urgent, because the water keeps flowing and the bill keeps rising.

Close the shutoff at the house and call us. If water is surfacing at the meter box, call the city as well. We take emergency water line calls 24/7 and book same-day appointments when available.

Signs of a failing service line

Unexplained wet ground

A soggy spot, a green stripe in dry grass or a patch of settled soil along the line route.

Pressure that dropped everywhere

If hot and cold taps are weak at every fixture, the supply is losing water before it reaches the house.

Discolored water

Brown or cloudy water after a long idle period can mean corroded pipe or a break letting soil in.

A bill that keeps rising

A hidden break wastes water day and night. Run the meter test to confirm.

Where a home has a galvanized steel service line, it corrodes from the inside, narrowing the line and cutting pressure. If your pressure has dropped slowly over years, the old line is likely clogged with scale. A replacement restores both flow and reliability.

Locating the line and calling 811

Before any digging we locate the line and have underground utilities marked through the free Texas811 service, so we do not hit gas, power or sewer lines. Gas, power and sewer lines all cross residential lots, so the call is not optional.

For a leak close to the meter, the city's Public Works office handles repairs on the city side. Call 281-478-7270 in business hours or 281-479-1511 after hours. We repair from the meter to your house.

Low pressure that is not a break

Not every pressure problem is a leak. A partly closed valve, a failing pressure regulator or a clogged fixture screen can all cut flow. We check pressure at an outdoor spigot with a gauge and test the regulator before recommending a line replacement.

Source note: how Deer Park gets its water

The City of Deer Park buys untreated surface water from the City of Houston through the Coastal Water Authority and treats it at its own plant. The city also keeps three standby wells in the Gulf Coast Aquifer for use if the raw supply is interrupted. A low-pressure event citywide is a city event, but a pressure drop in one house is almost always a house issue.

Know your shutoff before you need it

Walk outside and find the meter box and the house shutoff today. Many homeowners learn where the valve is only when water is already running. A stuck valve is easy to replace on a calm afternoon and very hard to fight at midnight.

Write the Public Works numbers, 281-478-7270 for business hours and 281-479-1511 after hours, on the inside of a cabinet door near the water heater.
