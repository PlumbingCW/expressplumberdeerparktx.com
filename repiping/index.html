When repair stops making sense

One pinhole leak is a repair. Three in a year is a pattern. Galvanized steel and older copper pipe wear from the inside, so a second leak is usually near. If you are paying for patches every few months, a repipe replaces the supply system and ends the cycle.

A pipe that has failed and is flooding the house is an emergency. See burst pipe repair. Our 24/7 line dispatches same-day when appointments are available, and repiping can be scheduled afterward.

Signs your supply lines are near the end

Repeated pinhole leaks

Small leaks at different points on the same line. Each repair buys limited time.

Discolored water

Brown or rust-colored water after sitting means iron pipe is corroding.

Weak pressure that is not a regulator

Galvanized pipe narrows as scale builds up on the inside.

Pipe age

Homes with galvanized steel or early copper supply lines can be at the end of their service life, and a plumber can check the material.

Most whole-house repipes today use PEX because it threads through walls with fewer cuts. Copper remains a choice for owners who want it. We explain the pros and cons for your house and let you choose.

Permits for re-piping in Deer Park

Source note: permit and inspection

The City of Deer Park names re-piping as plumbing work that requires a permit. The Public Works Permit Office at City Hall handles it, and the city says working without a required permit costs $500 plus triple the permit fee. We pull the permit for each repipe and arrange the inspection.

Source type: City of Deer Park Building Permits page

Older Deer Park homes and what is behind the wall

The city marks one 1928 home as its oldest, and it does not publish the pipe material of its housing. In an older house the original water lines may be galvanized or early copper, and the drain lines are a separate system. A repipe replaces the supply side. If the drains are original too, a camera inspection shows whether they need work.

Planning both together is often cheaper than doing them separately, because walls and floors only need to be opened once.

Living through a repipe

Preparing the house

  • ✓Clear closets, walls and cabinets where pipe will run, so the crew can reach them.
  • ✓Store drinking water and plan meals for the day the water is off.
  • ✓Move wall art and fragile items away from walls being opened.
  • ✓Tell us about any fixtures you plan to replace, so we connect the right ones while the walls are open.

Most owners notice stronger pressure and cleaner water after a repipe, because the old narrowed or corroded lines are gone. We flush the new lines before turning every fixture back on, so debris does not clog aerators. Keep the inspection sign-off with your house records. It helps at resale.
