When a failing water heater is an emergency

A cold shower is an inconvenience. A tank leaking onto a closet floor is not. If water is pooling around the base, if you smell gas near a gas unit or if the relief valve is running hot water, call the emergency line right away.

For a gas smell, leave the house first and call the gas utility or 911. Only after you are outside is it time to call a plumber. For a leaking tank, close the cold inlet valve on top of the heater and turn the power off at the breaker for an electric unit, or turn a gas unit to its pilot setting.

Why your hot water stopped

Pilot or igniter trouble

A gas heater that will not light may have a worn thermocouple or igniter. Both are inexpensive parts and a common same-visit repair.

Failed heating element

An electric heater with lukewarm water often has one of its two elements burned out. The upper and lower elements each have their own thermostat.

Sediment in the tank

Rumbling and popping mean mineral and debris layered on the bottom. A flush helps, though it will not rescue a rusted tank.

A leak from the tank itself

Water seeping from the shell means the steel inside has corroded through. That is a water heater replacement, not a repair.

Repair or replace: a decision framework

Repair makes sense when

  • The tank is under about ten years old and not leaking
  • The problem is a single part such as an element or thermostat
  • Hot water recovery is still strong
  • The repair costs far less than a new unit

Replace when

  • Water is leaking from the tank body
  • The unit is past ten to twelve years and has needed repeated repairs
  • The bottom of the tank is heavy with sediment
  • A repair would cost a large fraction of a new heater

We tell you which side of that line your heater sits on. If it is a replacement job, the price comparison between one more repair and a new unit is part of the conversation, not a surprise at the end.

What we check on a repair visit

1

Safety first

We check for gas leaks, scorch marks and water around the base before touching any control.

2

Test the heat source

Burner, igniter and thermocouple on gas units, elements and thermostats on electric units.

3

Check the relief valve

A temperature and pressure relief valve that drips or runs hot can mean excess pressure, and it is a safety device we do not ignore.

4

Check the anode and flush

A depleted anode rod speeds corrosion. We explain whether a flush or a rod swap is worth doing on your tank.

Permits and local rules for water heaters in Deer Park

The City of Deer Park lists water heaters under its plumbing permit requirements, along with re-piping, sewer work and irrigation. A simple part swap, such as an element or a thermocouple, is a repair. Replacing the whole unit is permitted work.

Homeowners can pull a plumbing permit on a primary residence with a Harris County homestead exemption, but if a contractor does the work, the contractor must pull it. The city's penalty for unpermitted work is $500 and triple the permit fee, so we handle the permit when a visit turns into a replacement.

Source note: chlorine and your water heater

Deer Park's 2023 Water Quality Report lists chlorine as the disinfectant in the city's water, with a total chlorine average of 1.99 ppm. It does not publish a hardness figure. Because hardness is unreported, we do not assume scale buildup. We look at the actual tank during the visit.
