Sewer backup: what to do first

Sewage is a health hazard, so keep children and pets out of the affected area and do not wade through standing water. Stop running water, including toilets, showers and the washer. Open windows if the smell is strong.

We take sewer emergencies 24/7 and book same-day appointments when available. Because the City of Deer Park asks you to call Public Works first about a sewer problem, make that call at the same time, on 281-478-7270 in business hours or 281-479-1511 after hours. The city checks its main, and if it is clear, the problem is yours.

Signs the line itself is damaged

Clogs that return within weeks

A cable clears the stoppage but the same one forms again. A damaged joint or belly in the line catches paper and grease.

Gurgling and slow drains throughout

When more than one fixture is slow, the problem is in the main.

A sunken or green strip of lawn

A leaking line saturates the soil. Grass above it grows faster or the ground drops where soil washes into the pipe.

Foul smells with no source

Sewer gas near a floor drain or in a yard can mark a break or a dry trap.

Trenchless methods spare your lawn and driveway but depend on the pipe's condition. The camera tells us whether lining is possible. If the pipe has collapsed or has a severe offset, digging is the only reliable repair.

Older neighborhoods and what is under the slab

The city does not publish what its sewer laterals are made of. In older homes they may be clay or cast iron, and on slab foundations many pass under the house before reaching the cleanout or street. Clay joints let roots in. Cast iron corrodes at the bottom of the pipe where flow is slowest.

When the damaged section is under the slab, we discuss tunneling beneath it, a reroute around the house or a liner. Each has a different cost and disruption. A camera run before any quote lets the numbers rest on what is really in the ground.

Permits for sewer work in Deer Park

Source note: sewer work needs a permit

The City of Deer Park lists sewer work among the jobs that require a plumbing permit, along with water heaters and re-piping. The city says unpermitted work carries a $500 penalty plus triple the permit fee under Ordinance 3392. We pull the permit for sewer repairs and replacements. Contractors must also be registered with the city and carry liability insurance.
