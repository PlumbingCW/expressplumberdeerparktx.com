Start with what is actually in your water

Deer Park's drinking water is surface water the city buys from Houston through the Coastal Water Authority and treats at its own plant. The city's 2023 Water Quality Report lists chlorine as the disinfectant, with a total chlorine average of 1.99 ppm and a range from 0.45 to 3.8 ppm. It does not publish a hardness figure.

That means we cannot tell you your hardness from the city report. A simple test strip or a lab test on your own tap answers the question, and it is the right place to begin before you buy any equipment.

Whole-house or point-of-use

Whole-house systems

  • Treat every tap, shower and appliance
  • Need space at the water entry and a drain for backwash or regeneration
  • Higher upfront cost
  • Filter and media changes are scheduled

Point-of-use systems

  • Treat the kitchen tap or a single fixture
  • Install under the sink with a small footprint
  • Lower upfront cost
  • Do not treat showers, laundry or other taps

Many homes combine the two, using a whole-house carbon filter for chlorine and a small RO unit at the kitchen sink for drinking water. We help you choose what fits the problem rather than selling the largest system.

Service and repair

Softeners that stop softening often have a stuck valve, a salt bridge in the brine tank or a failed controller. Systems that overflow from the brine line have clogged injectors or drain lines. We service conditioners and repair or replace failed parts. If your unit is old, we compare repair and replacement cost.

Source note: where to get a water report

The City of Deer Park publishes annual water quality reports on its website, and the Public Works staff listed in the report can answer source-water questions. Hardness, pH and alkalinity were not reported in the 2023 edition, so a test of your own tap remains the practical route.

Maintenance you should expect

Typical upkeep by system

  • ✓Carbon filters: change the cartridge on the maker's schedule, because a spent carbon filter stops working and can release what it caught.
  • ✓Reverse osmosis: change pre-filters yearly and the membrane every few years.
  • ✓Softeners: add salt, clean the brine tank occasionally and watch for salt bridges.
  • ✓Sediment filters: change when pressure drops or the cartridge looks dirty.

Write the install date and change dates on the unit or in a phone note. Most poor results from home water treatment come from neglected maintenance, not from the equipment.
